There is no 9:00am to 5:00pm business hours overlap between London and Sydney, in winter or in summer. When London offices open in the morning, Sydney's working day is already over. When Sydney starts its morning, London is asleep.

Pretending that a natural synchronous workday connects the UK and Australia leads to chronic fatigue, missed deliverables, and operational friction. Establishing the **best time meeting London Sydney** requires accepting this geographical reality and organizing around the least-bad working windows.

Every window in this guide is arithmetic computed directly from official UTC offsets and verified against standard operating practices. This guide details why the **London Sydney overlap** does not exist during normal office hours, outlines the primary morning-evening window, evaluates the reverse window, and provides a sustainable asynchronous framework for distributed teams.

## Why There is No Overlap Between London and Sydney

The **UK Australia meeting time** challenge is pure arithmetic: the gap is 11 hours in winter and 9 hours in summer &mdash; larger than an 8-hour working day.

9:00am London is 8:00pm Sydney in winter (GMT = UTC+0, AEDT = UTC+11), so by the time London starts work, Sydney is already past 5:00pm AEDT. When London finishes work at 5:00pm GMT, it is 4:00am AEDT the next day in Sydney. Conversely, when Sydney starts work at 9:00am AEDT, it is 10:00pm GMT the previous evening in London.

Teams create a functional bridge during the least-bad window: **7:00am to 9:00am GMT in London = 6:00pm to 8:00pm AEDT in Sydney**.

While still preventing any 9:00am to 5:00pm overlap, the least-bad window shifts to: **7:00am to 9:00am BST in London = 4:00pm to 6:00pm AEST in Sydney**.

## The Reverse Window: Sydney Morning Option

The reverse window &mdash; Sydney 7:00am&ndash;9:00am local time &mdash; is a second option for teams that prefer a Sydney morning over a Sydney evening:
- **Northern Winter:** Sydney 7:00am&ndash;9:00am AEDT = **London 8:00pm&ndash;10:00pm GMT the previous day**.
- **Northern Summer:** Sydney 7:00am&ndash;9:00am AEST = **London 10:00pm&ndash;12:00am BST the previous day**.

While convenient for Sydney staff beginning their day, it requires a late night in London, so it is not the first choice for recurring collaboration.

## The Daylight Saving Note: Opposite Hemispheres

A vital factor in UK-Australia scheduling is the seasonal inversion between the hemispheres: **Australia's daylight saving runs opposite to the northern hemisphere.**

Sydney is on AEDT (UTC+11) from Oct to Apr while London is on GMT, and on AEST (UTC+10) from Apr to Oct while London is on BST. That is why the gap is 11 hours in the northern winter and 9 hours in the northern summer, and why the Sydney-side time in the least-bad window moves two hours between seasons even though the London-side time does not.

A wall-clock time does not move; only the offset shifts:
- **8:00am London GMT = 7:00pm AEDT Sydney (winter)**; **8:00am London BST = 5:00pm AEST Sydney (summer)**.
- **7:00pm Sydney AEDT = 8:00am London GMT (winter)**; **5:00pm Sydney AEST = 8:00am London BST (summer)**.

## Rotating and Async Advice for Distributed Teams

Because no standard 9:00am to 5:00pm **Sydney London business hours** overlap exists, most London–Sydney teams should not meet daily &mdash; use recorded video, shared docs and decisions in writing for most things, and keep the live call to once or twice a week.

Rotate the burden: alternate the London-early-morning slot with the Sydney-early-morning (London-late-night) slot, and agree the rota in writing before it starts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there really no overlap between London and Sydney?
Yes. There is no standard 9:00am–5:00pm business hours overlap between London and Sydney in either season. The time gap is 11 hours in the northern winter and 9 hours in the northern summer, exceeding an 8-hour working day.

### What is the best time for a London–Sydney meeting?
The best time is **8:00am GMT (7:00pm AEDT)** in winter and **8:00am BST (5:00pm AEST)** in summer. This sits inside the least-bad window where London starts slightly early and Sydney is in early evening.

### What about the reverse window — Sydney morning?
The reverse window is Sydney 7:00am–9:00am, which corresponds to London 8:00pm–10:00pm GMT the previous evening in winter and 10:00pm–12:00am BST the previous evening in summer. It is a secondary option, but requires late nights in London.

### Does daylight saving time change the meeting window?
Australia's daylight saving runs opposite to the northern hemisphere. Sydney is on AEDT (UTC+11) when London is on GMT (11-hour gap), and on AEST (UTC+10) when London is on BST (9-hour gap), moving Sydney meeting times by two hours between seasons.

### What time is 9am in London in Sydney?
9:00am GMT in London is **8:00pm AEDT in Sydney** during winter. In summer, 9:00am BST in London is **6:00pm AEST in Sydney**.

### What time is 9am in Sydney in London?
9:00am AEDT in Sydney is **10:00pm GMT the previous day in London** during winter. In summer, 9:00am AEST in Sydney is **12:00am BST (midnight) the previous day in London**.

### How often should a London–Sydney team meet live?
Teams should meet live once or twice per week, using asynchronous video updates, shared documents, and written handoffs for daily progress to avoid time-zone burnout.
