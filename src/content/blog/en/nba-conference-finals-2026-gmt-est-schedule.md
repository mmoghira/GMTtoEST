## EDT vs GMT: Why the Offset Is 4 Hours

NBA playoff schedules usually show times as **ET**, but in May the correct Eastern Time abbreviation is **EDT**, not **EST**.

**EST** is Eastern Standard Time, which is UTC-5 and applies during the winter standard-time period. **EDT** is Eastern Daylight Time, which is UTC-4 and applies from March through early November.

That means the 2026 NBA Conference Finals use this simple conversion:

- **EDT to GMT: add 4 hours**
- **GMT to EDT: subtract 4 hours**

For example, an **8:30 p.m. EDT** tip-off becomes **00:30 GMT the next day**. An **8:00 p.m. EDT** tip-off becomes **00:00 GMT at midnight the next day**.

## Local Viewing Times Outside the US

**United Kingdom and Ireland:** Most 8:00 p.m. EDT games begin at 1:00 a.m. BST or IST, while 8:30 p.m. EDT games begin at 1:30 a.m.

**Germany, France, Spain and Central Europe:** Central European Summer Time is GMT+2, so games usually begin at 2:00 a.m. or 2:30 a.m.

**Pakistan:** PKT is GMT+5, putting the Conference Finals in the 5:00-5:30 a.m. window.

**India:** IST is GMT+5:30, so 8:00 p.m. EDT games start at 5:30 a.m. and 8:30 p.m. EDT games start at 6:00 a.m.

**Australia East:** AEST is GMT+10, making most Conference Finals games a mid-morning start at 10:00 a.m. or 10:30 a.m.
