## Why the Major Social Media Studies Disagree

Leading marketing technology platforms analyze millions—or billions—of published updates each year. Marketers often wonder why Sprout Social, Buffer, Vista Social, and Hootsuite arrive at conflicting conclusions.

The differences stem directly from **what each study measures**:

| Research Source | Dataset Scale | Collection Period | Primary Metric | Time-Zone Alignment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sprout Social (2026)** | ~2 billion engagements | 27 Nov 2025 – 27 Feb 2026 | Total Engagement Volume | Audience Local Time |
| **Buffer (2026)** | 52+ million posts | Jan 2024 – Dec 2025 | Median Engagement Rate | Creator Local Time |
| **Vista Social (2027)** | 11.4 million posts | Nov 2025 – Oct 2026 | Total Organic Impressions | Brand Local Time |
| **Hootsuite (2026)** | 1+ million posts | Multi-year sample (118 countries) | Engagement Rate | Time-Zone Agnostic |

- **Total Engagement Volume (Sprout Social):** Favors busy peak hours when aggregate likes, comments, and shares reach maximum levels.
- **Median Engagement Rate (Buffer):** Favors quieter hours where lower publishing competition allows individual posts to stand out.
- **Total Organic Impressions (Vista Social):** Favors low-competition hours when commercial brand publishing drops by 80% on weekends.
- **Time-Zone Agnostic Normalization (Hootsuite):** Blends global activity across 118 nations to create a generalized schedule.

## Best Times to Post by Region

Audience engagement is deeply influenced by regional working hours, commuting schedules, and cultural habits.

### United States

A survey of 1,100+ US marketers conducted by HubSpot (measuring marketer perception rather than behavioral data) found that Facebook and YouTube perform best between 9am and 12pm local time, Instagram peaks from 12pm to 3pm local time, and TikTok and X excel from 3pm to 6pm local time. National campaigns should anchor to US Eastern Time to reach multiple domestic time zones.

### United Kingdom

Sprout Social's study analyzing 230 million UK engagements (times reported in GMT) identified Thursday from 4pm to 11pm GMT as the week's strongest window, while Saturday is the quietest. Platform breakdowns: Facebook Tue–Thu 3pm to 9pm GMT; Instagram Mon–Thu 5pm to 9pm GMT (Sunday is the best day overall for Instagram); LinkedIn Thu 11am to 7pm GMT and Fri 9am to 5pm GMT; TikTok Wed 3pm to 11pm GMT and Mon/Thu 4pm to 12am GMT; Pinterest Thu 11am to 1pm GMT, 2pm to 5pm GMT, and 7pm to 9pm GMT. The **best time to post on social media UK** centers on late afternoon and evening relaxation.

### Canada

No dedicated empirical study was found for Canadian social media timing. Marketers should manage Canadian audiences by province, aligning schedules with adjacent US zones: Ontario and Quebec follow US Eastern Time; Manitoba aligns with Central Time; Alberta follows Mountain Time; and British Columbia mirrors Pacific Time. ## The Time-Zone Strategy: Converting Global Recommendations

Advising creators to simply "post at 12pm local time" is unhelpful when managing an international audience. If your business operates in New York but your followers reside in London, publishing at 12pm in your local time reaches British followers at 5pm GMT as they leave work. If your followers live in Sydney, that same post lands at 4am AEDT while they sleep.

Understanding the relationship between Eastern Standard Time (EST) and Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the fundamental anchor for international social media scheduling.

### The Multi-Region Rule

A mathematical reality of planetary geography is that **one post cannot hit the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia at the same time**.

In standard winter time, the gap between New York (EST) and London (GMT) is 5 hours, while the gap between New York (EST) and Sydney (AEDT) is 16 hours (14 hours during North American summer). If you publish at 2pm EST, it is 7pm GMT in London (prime evening leisure) but 6am AEDT the next morning in Sydney. If you publish at 8pm AEST for Sydney's evening, it is 5am EST in New York and 10am GMT in London.

**The Multi-Region Strategy:** If your brand maintains substantial audiences in North America, Europe, and Australia, you must post twice per cycle, spaced approximately 8 hours apart. For deeper mechanics, review [what is EST](/blog/what-is-est/) and [GMT vs EST](/blog/gmt-vs-est/).

### The Daylight Saving Time (DST) Trap

**DST does not change human biological behavior.** People still wake up at 7am local clock time, eat lunch at 12:30pm local clock time, and relax at 8pm local clock time. What changes is the **mathematical offset** between international time zones because nations shift clocks on different calendar dates:

- **Spring 2026:** US starts EDT on March 8, 2026; UK starts BST on March 29, 2026 (a 3-week gap where London-NY is 4 hours, not 5 hours).
- **Autumn 2026:** UK ends BST on October 25, 2026; US ends EDT on November 1, 2026 (a 1-week gap where London-NY is 4 hours).
- **Spring 2027:** US starts EDT on March 14, 2027; UK starts BST on March 28, 2027 (a 2-week gap where the offset is 4 hours instead of 5 hours).
- **Australia (New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, Tasmania, ACT):** daylight saving ends Sunday 4 April 2027.
- **Autumn 2027:** UK ends BST on October 31, 2027; US ends EDT on November 7, 2027 (a 1-week gap where the offset is 4 hours).

If your queue is anchored to GMT but your audience is in New York, posts publish one hour early during these periods. For full details, see [EST vs EDT](/blog/est-vs-edt/) and [what is daylight saving time](/blog/what-is-daylight-saving-time/).

## Impressions vs Engagement: The Sunday Paradox Explained

Vista Social's 2027 Report identifies Sunday as a top day for impressions, while Sprout Social identifies Sunday as the worst day of the week for total engagement. Understanding platform algorithms explains this apparent contradiction.

Across major social platforms, brand posting volume drops sharply on weekends. Multiple publishing studies confirm that **only 14% to 22% of total weekly brand posts are published on Saturday and Sunday**. Enterprise social teams operate primarily on Monday-through-Friday schedules, leaving weekend feeds largely empty of commercial content.

When brand publishing volume declines by 80%, feed competition drops dramatically. Social algorithms still require content to display to active users scrolling on their phones. Consequently, the few brand posts published on Sunday face minimal competition, capturing substantial algorithmic reach and impression delivery (+20% impressions on Threads, +16% on YouTube and X, and +13% on Facebook according to Vista Social).

- **Sunday maximizes Raw Impressions & Reach:** Ideal for top-of-funnel brand awareness, video storytelling, and consumer lifestyle content.
- **Tuesday–Thursday maximizes Active Engagement & Conversions:** Ideal for lead generation, link clicks, product launches, comment discussions, and B2B campaigns.

## How to Find Your Brand's Best Time to Post

Industry benchmarks provide a valuable starting point, but every brand audience possesses unique habits. Use native platform analytics tools to identify when your specific followers are online, then execute a structured four-week testing plan.

### Accessing Native Platform Analytics

- **Meta Business Suite (Facebook & Instagram):** Navigate to *Insights* > *Audience* > *Active Hours* for a follower scrolling heatmap in your local time.
- **TikTok Analytics:** In TikTok Studio, select *Analytics* > *Followers* > *Follower Activity* for an hourly activity graph.
- **YouTube Studio:** Navigate to *Analytics* > *Audience* > *"When your viewers are on YouTube"* to check peak viewer density.
- **LinkedIn Page Analytics:** Go to Page Admin Center > *Analytics* > *Updates* to evaluate engagement rates across publishing timestamps.
- **X Analytics:** Review your post analytics dashboard to track impressions, engagements, and clicks relative to tweet publication hours.
- **Pinterest Analytics:** Select *Analytics* > *Audience Insights* to review when followers interact with your creative categories.

### The 4-Week Empirical Testing Framework

1. **Week 1 (Baseline Audit):** Review your previous 90 days of publishing data across networks to identify top posts and audience time zones.
2. **Week 2 (Midday vs Evening Split-Test):** Compare midday engagement against evening engagement by publishing matched creative at 12pm local time and 6pm local time on alternating weekdays.
3. **Week 3 (Weekday vs Sunday Test):** Evaluate the Sunday paradox by publishing one asset on Wednesday at 1pm local time and a comparable asset on Sunday at 7pm local time.
4. **Week 4 (Schedule Lockdown):** Normalize metrics by dividing interactions by impressions to compute true engagement rates, then program winning slots into your scheduling software.
