Finding the **best time meeting New York London** requires no guesswork. Every window in this guide is arithmetic computed from coordinated universal time offsets and verified against standard 9:00am to 5:00pm business hours. This guide provides the exact **New York London overlap** for winter, summer, and the two annual daylight saving gap weeks.

## How the Overlap is Computed

Calculating the **London New York meeting time** uses a standard arithmetic method: intersect the two 9:00am to 5:00pm local business days. 9:00am New York is 2:00pm London in winter (EST = GMT&minus;5) and 2:00pm London in summer (EDT = GMT&minus;4, BST = GMT+1), so the window is the same in both seasons.

| City & Zone | Winter Offset | Summer Offset | Local Business Hours | Winter UTC Window | Summer UTC Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New York (EST / EDT)** | UTC&minus;5 (EST) | UTC&minus;4 (EDT) | 9:00am&ndash;5:00pm EST / EDT | 14:00&ndash;22:00 UTC | 13:00&ndash;21:00 UTC |
| **London (GMT / BST)** | UTC+0 (GMT) | UTC+1 (BST) | 9:00am&ndash;5:00pm GMT / BST | 09:00&ndash;17:00 UTC | 08:00&ndash;16:00 UTC |
| **Difference** | **5 Hours** | **5 Hours** | &mdash; | **Shared: 14:00&ndash;17:00 UTC** | **Shared: 13:00&ndash;16:00 UTC** |

Because both cities advance their wall clocks by 60 minutes in summer, the net difference remains 5 hours. To learn more about regional time zones, see our guides on [what is EST](/blog/what-is-est/), [GMT vs EST](/blog/gmt-vs-est/), and [US time zones](/blog/us-time-zones/).

## The Two Gap Weeks: 25 Oct–1 Nov 2026 and 14–28 Mar 2027

While summer and winter maintain a consistent 5-hour difference, the calendar experiences two transitional periods each year: 25 Oct–1 Nov 2026 and 14–28 Mar 2027.

These gap weeks exist because the United States and the United Kingdom change their clocks on different Sundays. The US follows the Energy Policy Act of 2005 (second Sunday in March to first Sunday in November), while the UK follows European harmonization dates (last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October). During these weeks, one side has changed its clocks and the other has not, so the gap between New York and London contracts to 4 hours instead of 5 hours.

With a 4-hour offset, intersecting 9:00am–5:00pm EDT in New York with 9:00am–5:00pm GMT in London produces an expanded 4-hour window: **9:00am–1:00pm New York (EDT) = 1:00pm–5:00pm London (GMT)**.

However, this change requires caution for standing meetings:

> [!WARNING]
> A recurring meeting fixed at 2:00pm London time is **10:00am in New York** during these gap weeks instead of 9:00am. Attendees who expect 9:00am EDT will arrive an hour early, or miss the call if unaware of the shift.

Conversely, a meeting fixed at 9:00am New York time is 1:00pm in London during these weeks instead of 2:00pm GMT. For broader scheduling frameworks across global teams, see our pillar guide on [how to schedule a meeting across time zones](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-meeting-across-time-zones/).

## Best and Worst Times to Book

Selecting the right meeting slot within the overlap ensures productive discussions and respects employee schedules:

- **Best Time &mdash; 10:00am New York = 3:00pm London:** This sits in the middle of the overlap when both sides are alert. New York team members have arrived at 9:00am EST / EDT and settled in, while London staff have finished lunch and retain two afternoon hours to implement decisions before 5:00pm GMT / BST.
- **Secondary Window &mdash; 11:00am New York = 4:00pm London:** Inside the overlap, suitable for 60-minute presentations or technical workshops.
- **Worst Time &mdash; Before 9:00am New York:** An 8:00am EST meeting requires New York attendees to join before regular business hours. By this point, London is already past 2:00pm GMT and running out of day while New York is rushed.
- **Worst Time &mdash; After 12:00pm New York:** Booking at 1:00pm EST places the call at 6:00pm GMT in London. London employees are past 5:00pm GMT, infringing on personal evening time.

The single **best time to schedule a meeting London New York** is therefore 10:00am EST / EDT in New York (3:00pm GMT / BST in London). For broader European collaboration, see our guide on the [best time for US and Europe meetings](/best-time-us-europe-meetings/).

## The DST Trap: March 2027 Worked Example

The most common transatlantic scheduling error is misunderstanding daylight saving time: **a wall-clock time does not move, only the offset shifts.**

When an invitation specifies 2:00pm London time, London's clock stays at 2:00pm. But because the US and UK change clocks on different dates, the offset shifts between 5 hours and 4 hours.

Consider a standing meeting scheduled at **2:00pm London time** across March 2027:

1. **Before 14 March 2027 (Winter):** London is on GMT (UTC+0) and New York is on EST (UTC-5). A meeting at 2:00pm London is **9:00am New York**.
2. **14 March to 28 March 2027 (Gap Weeks):** New York advances to EDT (UTC-4) on 14 March 2027, while London remains on GMT (UTC+0). A meeting at 2:00pm London is **10:00am New York**.
3. **After 28 March 2027 (Summer):** London advances to BST (UTC+1) on 28 March 2027, while New York remains on EDT (UTC-4). A meeting at 2:00pm London returns to **9:00am New York**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best time for a meeting between New York and London?
The best time is **10:00am EST (3:00pm GMT)** in winter and **10:00am EDT (3:00pm BST)** in summer. This sits in the middle of the overlap when both sides are alert during regular business hours.

### How many hours do New York and London overlap?
New York and London overlap for **3 hours** (9:00am–12:00pm New York = 2:00pm–5:00pm London) during winter and summer, expanding to **4 hours** (9:00am–1:00pm New York = 1:00pm–5:00pm London) during the two gap weeks.

### Is the overlap the same in summer and winter?
Yes. In winter, New York is UTC-5 (EST) and London is UTC+0 (GMT), a 5-hour gap. In summer, New York is UTC-4 (EDT) and London is UTC+1 (BST), keeping the exact same 5-hour gap.

### How do I schedule a recurring meeting between New York and London?
