## Why the Studies Disagree: Methodology and Metrics

The contradiction between reports comes down to the distinct metrics each research team measures:

* **Total engagement volume favours busy hours:** Sprout Social tracks gross volume, peaking weekday afternoons (2:00 PM to 6:00 PM local time) as traffic builds.
* **Median engagement rate favours quiet hours:** Buffer measures rate across 7.1M TikTok posts; low-competition weekend mornings (Sunday 9:00 AM local time) yield higher ratios.
* **Views and impressions favour evening leisure:** Metricool views peak from 6:00 PM to 9:00 PM local time (peak 8:00 PM local time); Vista Social finds Saturday 9:00 PM to 12:00 AM local time gives +4% impressions.
* **Timing matters least on TikTok:** Vista Social found only ±3% variation across days (Sunday +3%). TikTok distributes videos by watch time and completion rate rather than timestamps.

## The Time-Zone Strategy: Converting TikTok Benchmarks

Publishing at "local time" fails across borders: 6:00 PM GMT in London is **1:00 PM EST** in New York, **10:00 AM PST** in Los Angeles, and **5:00 AM AEDT next day** in Sydney. Always anchor your schedule to your primary follower hub.

### The Multi-Region Rule: Why One Post Cannot Cover the Globe
No single post can hit the US, UK, and Australia simultaneously: **1:00 PM EST** is **6:00 PM GMT** in London, but **5:00 AM AEDT next day** in Sydney.
**The Multi-Region Rule:** To reach North America, Europe, and Australasia, **post twice per day, spaced roughly 8 hours apart** (e.g. 1:00 PM EST / 6:00 PM GMT, and 9:00 PM EST / 2:00 PM AEDT next day).

### The Daylight Saving Time Trap (2026 / 2027 Transition Dates)
Daylight saving shifts never change human scrolling routines; people browse when awake according to their local clocks. What changes is the **mathematical offset between international time zones**:
* **US & Canada DST:** Ended **Sunday, November 1, 2026**; resumes **Sunday, March 14, 2027** (EST GMT−5 shifts to EDT GMT−4).
* **UK & EU DST:** Ended **Sunday, October 25, 2026**; resumes **Sunday, March 28, 2027** (GMT shifts to BST GMT+1).
* **Australia (NSW/VIC/SA/TAS/ACT) DST:** Started **Sunday, October 4, 2026**; ends **Sunday, April 4, 2027** (AEDT GMT+11).

During the mismatch week of October 25 to November 1, 2026, London–New York shrank to **4 hours**. A schedule fixed in your own zone breaks by one hour at each change: 2:00 PM GMT = 9:00 AM EST in winter, but 10:00 AM EDT in summer. ## How to Find Your Best Time to Post on TikTok

Your profile's native analytics should guide your ongoing posting schedule:

### Step 1: Analyze TikTok Native Analytics
In TikTok, go to **Profile > Creator Tools > Analytics > Followers**. Check the **Follower Activity** graph by hours and days. Post **30 to 60 minutes before peak activity** so the algorithm indexes captions and metadata before traffic swells.

### Step 2: Execute a 4-Week Empirical Split Test
Run a 4-week split test across three windows:
* **Slot A:** Tuesday at **2:00 PM local time** (Sprout volume).
* **Slot B:** Saturday at **9:00 AM local time** (Buffer engagement rate).
* **Slot C:** Thursday at **6:00 PM local time** (Metricool evening leisure).

Keep video format consistent. Compare **views, shares, and watch time / completion rate**, not likes alone, as TikTok's algorithm prioritizes retention and shares for FYP reach.
