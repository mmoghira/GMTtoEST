## EDT to GMT Conversion Rule for World Cup 2026

The most important rule for Eastern Time matches is:

**EDT to GMT: add 4 hours**

That means:

- **12:00 PM EDT = 16:00 GMT**
- **3:00 PM EDT = 19:00 GMT**
- **6:00 PM EDT = 22:00 GMT**
- **9:00 PM EDT = 01:00 GMT the next day**

The reason is daylight saving time. In June and July, New York, New Jersey, Philadelphia, Boston, Atlanta and Miami observe **Eastern Daylight Time, UTC-4**. Winter **Eastern Standard Time, UTC-5** is not the correct offset for these summer match dates.

## Best Viewing Windows by Region

### GMT Viewers

The most comfortable Eastern Time slot is **3:00 PM EDT**, which lands at **7:00 PM GMT**. Evening slots are still manageable, but a 9:00 PM EDT match becomes 1:00 AM GMT the next day.

### UK Viewers

The UK is on **BST, GMT+1** during the tournament. A 3:00 PM EDT kickoff is 8:00 PM BST, while a 9:00 PM EDT kickoff is 2:00 AM BST the next day.

### Pakistan and India Viewers

The noon Eastern slot is the friendliest window: **9:00 PM PKT** and **9:30 PM IST**. The 3:00 PM EDT slot is a midnight match in Pakistan and a 12:30 AM match in India.

### Australia Viewers

Australia gets many overnight and early-morning kickoffs, but the Final is much better than many US prime-time finals: **5:00 AM AEST on Monday, July 20**.

## Common Mistake: EST vs EDT

Many fans search for **World Cup 2026 EST to GMT**, but the tournament happens in summer. For Eastern Time venues, the correct label is usually **EDT**, not EST.

- EST is UTC-5 and applies in winter.
- EDT is UTC-4 and applies during daylight saving time.
- World Cup 2026 Eastern Time matches in June and July should be converted with the UTC-4 offset.

If you are converting from a ticket, broadcaster listing or calendar invite, use the date and venue city. The [GMT vs EST guide](/gmt-vs-est/) explains the difference in more detail.

## How to Watch Without Missing Kickoff

1. Check the official match listing and venue city.
2. Identify the venue timezone, such as EDT, CDT, CST or PDT.
3. Convert using the match date, not a generic winter offset.
4. Watch for **+1** in global tables because many conversions land after midnight.
5. Verify close to match day because official schedules and broadcast windows can change.
