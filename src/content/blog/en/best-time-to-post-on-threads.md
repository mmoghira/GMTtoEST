## Why the Studies Disagree: Methodology and Sample Differences

Benchmark reports diverge on the **best time to post on Threads** because each platform tracks different user actions across distinct datasets:

|**Hootsuite**|1M+ posts (118 countries)|Interaction volume & clicks|Tue 8am local; Wed 12pm local|Time-zone agnostic|
|**Vista Social (2027)**|11.4M posts (Nov 2025–Oct 2026)|Impressions & reach|Sun 9pm–12am local (+20% reach)|Brand local time|

Industry giant **Sprout Social does NOT cover Threads** in its research. Say this plainly: Sprout tracks zero Threads data, and must never be cited as a Threads source.

In plain words: **median engagement rate favours quiet morning hours** (7am–12pm local) when focused readers write replies during commutes. Conversely, **impressions favour low-competition Sunday night** (9pm–12am local), when scrollers browse in bed while brand publishing ceases, boosting algorithmic reach.

### Wednesday

### Thursday
Thursday represents the clearest daytime consensus. Buffer names **Thursday at 9am local** its top slot for median engagement rate, and Hootsuite independently confirms **9am local**.

### Friday

### Saturday

### Sunday
Sunday illustrates contrasting metrics: Buffer rates Sunday lowest for engagement rate, and Hootsuite records **1pm local**. Meanwhile, Vista Social finds **Sunday 9pm to 12am local** delivers **+20% impressions** — the highest slot of any network. Report all three, labelled by metric (engagement vs impressions).

## The Time-Zone Strategy: Converting Slots Across Global Markets

Publishing at "local time" is an incomplete answer. A post published at **9am GMT** in London goes live at **4am EST** in New York, **1am PST** in San Francisco, and **8pm AEDT** in Sydney. Always anchor updates to your primary target audience hub.

### Master Threads Time Zone Conversion Table
Headline benchmark slots during winter standard time (1 Nov 2026–14 Mar 2027; **EST = GMT−5, PST = GMT−8, CET = GMT+1, IST = GMT+5:30, AEDT = GMT+11**):

|Headline Benchmark Slot|GMT (London)|EST (New York)|PST (San Francisco)|CET (Paris/Berlin)|IST (Mumbai)|AEDT (Sydney)|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**Buffer Morning Peak**|**9am GMT**|4am EST|1am PST|10am CET|2:30pm IST|8pm AEDT|
|**Hootsuite Midday Peak**|**12pm GMT**|7am EST|4am PST|1pm CET|5:30pm IST|11pm AEDT|
|**Vista Late-Night Peak**|**9pm GMT**|4pm EST|1pm PST|10pm CET|2:30am IST *(+1)*|8am AEDT *(+1)*|
|**US Morning Baseline**|2pm GMT|**9am EST**|6am PST|3pm CET|7:30pm IST|1am AEDT *(+1)*|

*(+1) indicates following calendar day.* Verify custom hours using our [GMT to EST converter](https://gmttoestconverter.com/).

### Worked Multi-Zone Examples
Headline slots across financial hubs demonstrate cross-border offsets:
*   **Buffer's Thu 9am London (GMT):** Equals **4am EST** New York, **1am PST** San Francisco, **10am CET** Paris, and **2:30pm IST** Mumbai.
*   **Vista's Sun 9pm–12am London (GMT):** Equals **4–7pm EST** New York, **1–4pm PST** California, and **2:30–5:30am IST** Mumbai next day.

To reach US East Coast scrollers during their 9am EST peak, UK and European creators must post at **2pm GMT**.

### The Multi-Region Rule: Post Twice, 8 Hours Apart
A single post cannot hit the US, UK, and Australia simultaneously. When New York is active at 9am EST, London is at 2pm GMT and Sydney is at 1am AEDT next day.

**Multi-Region Rule:** Publish fresh updates twice daily, roughly 8 hours apart: **2pm GMT / 9am EST** (US morning / European afternoon) and **10pm GMT / 5pm EST / 9am AEDT next day** (US evening / Sydney morning). Plan schedules with our [Meeting Planner](/meeting-planner/) and [GMT to EST Converter](/).

### The Daylight Saving Time (DST) Trap
Daylight Saving Time does not change when people scroll; audience habits stay fixed to local clock time. What changes is the **mathematical offset** between international zones:
*   **US & Canada:** DST ended Sun 1 Nov 2026; resumes Sun 14 Mar 2027.
*   **UK & Europe:** BST/CEST ended Sun 25 Oct 2026; resumes Sun 28 Mar 2027.
*   **Australia (NSW/VIC/SA/TAS/ACT):** DST began 4 Oct 2026, ends 4 Apr 2027.

During the gap week 25 Oct–1 Nov 2026, London was only 4 hours ahead of New York (2pm GMT was 10am EDT, not 9am EST). See [What Is EST](/blog/what-is-est/), [GMT vs EST](/blog/gmt-vs-est/), and [What Is Daylight Saving Time](/blog/what-is-daylight-saving-time/).

## How to Find Your Brand’s Best Time to Post on Threads for Maximum Engagement

Macro benchmark reports provide starting hypotheses, but your account requires empirical testing. Follow this 4-week framework using native platform analytics.

### Step 1: Audit Native Threads Analytics
Access Threads analytics via mobile or web (`threads.net`). Review top 20 posts from the past 90 days. Focus on **replies and reposts** rather than likes alone, as Meta's algorithm prioritizes conversation. Group top performers into morning (7–10am), midday (11am–2pm), and evening (7–10pm) cohorts in your audience's dominant zone.

### Step 2: The 4-Week Split Test Plan
Execute a controlled test comparing headline study benchmarks across identical formats:
*   **Week 1 (Thursday Morning Baseline):** Post Thursday at **9am audience local time** (Buffer benchmark).
*   **Week 2 (Tuesday Early Morning Test):** Post Tuesday at **8am audience local time** (Hootsuite benchmark).
*   **Week 3 (Wednesday Midday Peak):** Post Wednesday at **12pm audience local time** (Hootsuite midday benchmark).
*   **Week 4 (Sunday Night Impression Test):** Post Sunday at **9:30pm audience local time** (Vista impressions benchmark).

### Step 3: Evaluate Conversation Metrics
Evaluate performance using community metrics:
*   **Reply Rate:** Measures whether your prompt sparked active discussion.
*   **Repost Rate:** Signals content amplified into follower feeds.
*   **Avoid Likes Alone:** Likes are low-effort; Meta's algorithm prioritizes deep replies.
