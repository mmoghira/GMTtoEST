How Our Time Calculation Works

Understanding the mechanics behind time zones doesn't have to be complicated. Here is a beginner-friendly guide to the essential concepts that power accurate time conversions.

What is GMT?

Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the zero-meridian baseline originally established at the Royal Observatory in London. Used universally since 1884, it serves as the foundation for global timekeeping.

What is UTC?

Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the modern successor to GMT, meticulously maintained by atomic clocks. While scientifically distinct, it is practically identical to GMT for civilian use.

What is EST?

Eastern Standard Time (EST) operates at a UTC-5 offset. It is the standard time used by major hubs like New York, Toronto, and Miami from November to March.

What is EDT?

Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) uses a UTC-4 offset during the warmer months. It is active from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November.

What is a UTC Offset?

A UTC offset represents the specific number of hours added or subtracted from UTC to find local time. For example, EST is exactly 5 hours behind UTC (-5), while EDT is 4 hours behind (-4).

What is Daylight Saving Time?

Daylight saving time is a seasonal clock adjustment to maximize evening daylight. Clocks spring forward 1 hour in March and fall back in November across most of the US and Canada.
